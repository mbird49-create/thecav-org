## 2. One-line gloss

Resolve that draws on hidden reserves of energy to keep going, or act at all, when adversity has passed the point where stopping would seem reasonable. It can be admirable or harmful.

## 3. Etymology and semantic field

**Evidence.** Finland's Institute for the Languages of Finland (Kotus 2017), drawing on the etymological dictionary *Suomen sanojen alkuperä*, derives *sisu* from *sisä*, "inside." Its original sense was "interior, what is inside." In Mikael Agricola's sixteenth-century Finnish, *sisu* could mean the particular quality of a thing or, of a person, their nature or disposition.

**Evidence.** Old religious literature often spoke of *paha sisu*, "evil sisu," and from there the word came to mean malice (Kotus 2017). Daniel Juslenius's 1745 dictionary glosses *sisucunda* as the inner organs where the passions arise (Kotus 2017). Helminen's 2024 dissertation on the word's history confirms the early senses: inner part or entrails, nature or character, and malice or anger.

**Interpretation.** Sisu's semantic field is still inward and bodily: a force "in the guts," not a principle. That fits the visceral way present-day Finns describe it (section 4).

## 4. How it is practiced

**Evidence.** Lahti (2019) analyzed open-ended answers from a survey of 1,208 respondents. She found that the core of sisu is going beyond one's preconceived limits by tapping stored-up reserves of energy. In her words, it is "more about finding energy in the moment than about long-term endurance." Respondents described it as bodily and visceral rather than as conscious willpower.

**Evidence.** The validated Sisu Scale also measures a "harmful sisu" made up of three factors: harm to reason, harm to self, and harm to others (Henttonen et al. 2022).

**Evidence.** Sisu is used constantly in sport, war memory, work, and politics. The phrase "with sisu and heart" (*sisulla ja sydämellä*) has been a political slogan. Brands include a pastille, a truck maker, and sports clubs (Kotus 2017). In Finnish North America, sisu became a central marker of ethnic identity (Taramaa 2009).

**Interpretation.** Sisu is not a ritual. It is named after the fact ("that took sisu"), demanded ("show some sisu"), and criticized ("pigheaded sisu").

## 5. History and change over time

**Evidence (Helminen 2024).**
- The word was used for a Finnish regional community by the 1860s, and "Finnish sisu" appeared by the end of the nineteenth century, first in the press.
- International sporting success and the 1918 Finnish Civil War set off lively debate about sisu as national character in the 1920s.
- In the first half of the twentieth century, sisu's main job in public discourse was to build a unified nation. In the second half, emphasis moved to individual sisu and to worry that Finns no longer had enough of it.
- Sisu discourse was gendered throughout.

**Evidence.** Kotus (2017) notes that sports pioneer Lauri Pihkala's *Urheilun opas* (1920) included a chapter on sisu. It also records a 1942 newspaper competition, run by *Uusi Suomi*, for the best definition of sisu, which drew about 1,000 entries.

**Evidence.** In English, *Time* (8 January 1940), reporting on the Winter War, wrote that sisu "is a compound of bravado and bravery, of ferocity and tenacity, of the ability to keep fighting after most people would have quit."

**Evidence.** In 2017, Finland's centenary year, *sisu* was chosen as "Finland's word" (*Suomen sana*) in a public contest (Kotus 2017). The same post cites Saska Saarikoski in *Helsingin Sanomat* (2015): a far-right group named Suomen Sisu was founded in 1998.

**Evidence.** From 2019, sisu became a measurable psychological construct (Lahti 2019; Henttonen et al. 2022). Alava and Wolf-Meyer (2026) call this "paradoxical scientization." Their argument is that the research depends on sisu being uniquely Finnish and universal at the same time, and that this lays the ground for selling it as neoliberal self-help.

## 6. Why the generic English word fails

**Interpretation.**
- *Grit* (Duckworth) stresses sustained passion for long-term goals. Lahti's respondents describe sisu as acute: energy found in the moment, not a project plan.
- *Resilience* suggests bouncing back. Sisu is about pressing on before any recovery.
- *Toughness* and *stubbornness* each catch one sense, positive or negative. Sisu holds both, and Finnish speakers use it for both.

**Correction to the earlier catalog sketch.** The earlier sketch's gloss, "extraordinary perseverance after ordinary endurance has already run out," fits Lahti (2019) well. But the earlier sketch treats sisu's national standing as long-settled. The word's positive, national meaning is largely a product of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and its older senses were mostly negative (Helminen 2024).

## 7. Evidence on outcomes or social effects

- **Evidence.** Henttonen et al. (2022) built and validated an 18-item Sisu Scale across three samples (436, 423, and 82 participants). Beneficial sisu correlated with higher well-being (WHO-5, r = 0.41) and fewer depressive symptoms (r = −0.27). Harmful sisu correlated with lower well-being (r = −0.33), more depressive symptoms (r = 0.52), more work stress (r = 0.46), and more general stress (r = 0.39) in the 82-person worker sample.
- **Limits.** The data are cross-sectional and self-reported. The first two samples were mostly female university students. The design cannot establish causality.
- **Interpretation.** The strongest empirical finding is that the harmful side is real and measurable. That supports the earlier sketch's caution that sisu "can be admired and also costly."
- **Gap.** No study was found on sisu and collective outcomes, such as wartime cohesion or negotiation behavior **[uncertain]**.

## 8. Diplomatic and mediation relevance

**Interpretation.**
- In protracted talks or post-conflict settings, praising a party's sisu (or "resilience") can sound like a demand to absorb more harm. The harmful-sisu evidence suggests that mediators should also ask when stopping, or accepting help, would be the stronger choice.
- Appeals to national sisu carry Winter War memory. With Finnish counterparts that can build solidarity, but it can also frame compromise as weakness.

## 9. Cautions

- **Commercialization.** Sisu sells: candy, trucks, country branding, wellness guides (Helminen 2024; Kotus 2017). Alava and Wolf-Meyer (2026) tie its scientization to self-help markets.
- **Nationalism.** The word carries ultranationalist uses (Kotus 2017). Alava and Wolf-Meyer warn that sisu science sits alongside ethnonationalist mobilization and can reinforce racialized, ableist, and classist exclusions.
- **Gender.** Sisu discourse has been gendered throughout its modern history (Helminen 2024). Gender differences in Sisu Scale scores were not checked for this entry **[uncertain]**.
- **Romanticization.** **Interpretation.** Helminen's history shows that the national meaning was built in the press and public debate from the late nineteenth century. Framings of sisu as "timeless Finnish character" should be avoided.
- **Insider vs. outsider voice.** The main sources are Finnish (Lahti, Helminen, Henttonen et al., Kotus). The main critique (Alava and Wolf-Meyer 2026) comes from science and technology studies. CAV should avoid presenting popular English "sisu" books as evidence.
